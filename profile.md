# Reader profile

The reader is a developer with intermediate to advanced technical knowledge. They read articles in English and French; the language of an article does not affect its score.

## Core interests (score 8-10)

- **AI from a developer's point of view**: using, integrating and building with AI in code (APIs, SDKs, agents, coding tools, model capabilities that change how software is written).
- **Frontier AI models**: news about state-of-the-art models, but only when it is concrete (capabilities, API availability, pricing, limits, real impact on development). Marketing announcements with no substance score low.
- **AI regulation and law**: laws, bills and regulatory attempts about AI, especially in the EU, France and the US. Other countries count when the law is significant or novel. Political statements or rumors with no concrete text score lower.
- **Ecology and environmental impact of tech**: energy, water, carbon footprint, data centers, hardware lifecycle, sustainable software. AI-related environmental topics are the most interesting.
- **Game development technology**: engines, rendering, tooling and techniques for making games, with a focus on performance, web builds (WebGPU, WebAssembly, etc.) and open source. All engines and technologies are relevant.

Articles that combine two or more core interests (e.g. the carbon footprint of training a model, a Rust game engine running on the web) deserve the very top of the scale (9-10).

## Secondary interests (score 5-7)

- New developer technologies: web frameworks, tools, libraries, editor plugins and anything that makes development easier or faster.
- Business news about tech and AI (acquisitions, funding, revenue), in moderation: relevant only when it has a clear impact on the fields above.

## Languages and ecosystems

- **Rust** is a strong positive signal: it should noticeably raise the score of an otherwise relevant article.
- **C++** is a mild positive signal.
- Other languages are neutral.

## Preferred article formats

From most to least valued:

1. Experience reports and post-mortems.
2. Tutorials and practical guides (not beginner-level).
3. Opinion pieces, especially from practitioners and personal or engineering blogs.
4. Release announcements (new versions, new models, new tools), when they contain concrete details.
5. Business news (see secondary interests).
6. Benchmarks on their own are of little interest.

## Not interested (score 0-2)

- Cryptocurrency, blockchain, NFTs.
- Politics with no direct link to technology.
- Video game news about the games themselves (releases, reviews, sales), as opposed to how games are made.
- Content written for a general audience that does not know the technology (e.g. "Can ChatGPT replace your doctor?"), even if the topic is AI.
- Beginner-level introductions to technologies.
- Advertising or sponsored content disguised as an article, including promotion of developer tools.
